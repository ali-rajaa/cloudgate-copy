Why your backup may be quietly skipping files

Backup says complete but photos are missing? There are a handful of reasons a backup reports success while covering a fraction of your library. Here is each one, how to check if photos are backed up, and how to fix it.

13 min read Updated 29 September 2026 Android and iPhone Troubleshooting

The dangerous kind of backup failure does not announce itself. There is no error, warning badge or notification. The app says complete, the tick is green, and somewhere between a handful and most of your library was never uploaded. If you searched for why only some of your photos backed up, that gap is the reason.

People usually discover this at the worst possible moment: after a phone is lost, or partway through setting up a replacement, when they go looking for something specific and it is not there. By then the original is gone.

The causes are few and they repeat. Almost every case of a backup not backing up all photos comes down to one of six things, and each takes a minute to check. This guide covers all six, plus the one verification step that would have caught any of them.

If you have found some photos not backed up while others are fine, the pattern of what is missing usually identifies the cause on its own. A backup skipping files rarely does so at random, and knowing which category is absent points straight at which setting is responsible.

First: how to check if photos are backed up

Before diagnosing anything, establish whether you have a problem. This takes two minutes and is worth doing even if you think everything is fine.

The test is not the status label in the app. A status label is the app reporting on what it believes it was asked to do, which is exactly the thing in question. The test is opening your library from somewhere that is not the phone.

  • Open your account in a browser on a computer, not the app on the phone.
  • Scroll to the very beginning of your library and check your oldest photos are there.
  • Look for a photo somebody sent you rather than one you took, since those live in a different folder.
  • Find a screenshot from a few months ago.
  • Compare the rough count against what your phone's gallery reports.

If all five are present, your coverage is probably genuine. If any category is missing, that tells you which of the causes below applies, because each one fails in a characteristic way.

Checking from the phone proves nothing. The phone has its own copy of everything regardless of whether a single file was ever uploaded, so the photo will open perfectly either way.

Cause 1: limited photo access

The single most common reason a backup says complete but photos are missing, and the hardest to spot, because the app is behaving exactly as instructed.

When an app asks for your photos, modern phones offer a choice between granting access to everything and granting access to selected items. Selected access is a good privacy feature and the wrong setting for a backup.

If you tapped the selected option, perhaps months ago, perhaps without reading it, the app can only see the specific images you picked at that moment. It backs those up, finds nothing else to do, and reports complete. It is not lying. It has uploaded one hundred percent of what it is permitted to see.

How it fails: a very small number of photos backed up, often an oddly random selection, with a status of complete.

The fix: open your phone's settings, find the app, and change photo permission from selected or limited to full access. The rest of your library will begin uploading once it can be seen.

This resets more often than people expect. A major system update can revert app permissions, so recheck it after any large update rather than assuming a setting from last year is still in place.

Cause 2: folders that were never selected

Most backup tools default to the camera folder and nothing else. That covers what you photographed yourself and misses a large share of the images on your phone.

The folders that sit outside the camera roll:

  • Messaging media. Every photo and video anyone sent you, in the messaging app's own folder.
  • Screenshots. Tickets, confirmations, reference shots, all in a separate folder.
  • Downloads. Images saved from a browser or pulled out of an email.
  • Edited copies. Some gallery apps write edits to their own folder rather than overwriting the original.
  • Third party camera and social apps. Several write to directories of their own.

How it fails: your own photos are all present, but pictures other people sent you are absent. This is the version that causes the most distress, because messaging media is often where family photos taken on somebody else's phone live.

The fix: open the backup settings and go through the folder list deliberately, enabling everything you care about rather than accepting the default.

The photo backup guide covers folder coverage in more detail.

Cause 3: battery optimisation and power saving

This is the cause that makes a working backup stop months later, which is why people are certain they set it up correctly. They did. Something switched it off afterwards.

Phones aggressively restrict background activity to preserve battery. An app you have not opened in a few weeks gets treated as dormant and has its background permissions narrowed. For a backup app, background activity is the entire job.

Low power mode has the same effect deliberately, suspending background transfers until it is switched off. Some manufacturers layer a second power manager on top of the standard one, so an app can be exempted in one place and still restricted by the other.

How it fails: everything up to a certain date is backed up and nothing after it. The date is when the restriction kicked in.

The fix: set the app to unrestricted battery usage, allow background refresh, and on manufacturer skins check the separate device care or power manager as well. Then open the app to let it catch up.

The Android guide covers the separate power managers, and the iPhone guide covers background refresh and low power mode.

Cause 4: waiting for Wi-Fi that never comes

Wi-Fi only uploading is the default and it is usually the right setting. It becomes a problem for anyone who is rarely on a network.

If you mostly use mobile data, or you have been travelling, a Wi-Fi only backup will sit patiently doing nothing for as long as that lasts. The app looks enabled the entire time. Nothing is broken, it is waiting.

A partial version of this catches people out too: allowing photos over mobile data while restricting video to Wi-Fi. That is a sensible setting, and it means your video is queued rather than backed up until you next connect.

How it fails: the last backup date is weeks old with no error, or photos are current while videos lag behind.

The fix: connect to Wi-Fi and let it run, or allow mobile data for photos if you are rarely on a network. Check the last backup date rather than the status word, since waiting and finished can look similar at a glance.

Cause 5: the account is full

When storage runs out, uploads stop. Everything already stored stays exactly where it is and nothing is deleted, but new files stop arriving.

Notifications about this are easy to miss, and they arrive at the moment it happens rather than being repeated. Months later the app may show a stalled backup without making the reason obvious.

How it fails: a clean cut-off date with nothing after it, similar to the battery cause, but with a full or near-full storage figure in your account.

The fix: either clear space, usually by removing large videos you no longer need, or move to a larger plan. Uploads resume from where they stopped.

Cause 6: assuming a sync is a backup

This one is not a failure at all. It is a service working correctly while doing something different from what the person assumed.

Sync keeps the same set of files on several devices, so a deletion travels to every device just as faithfully as an addition. Backup keeps a copy that is unaffected by what happens to the original. People frequently have the first and believe they have the second.

There is a related trap. On some platforms, turning on photo syncing changes what a device backup contains, on the reasoning that the photos are already covered by the sync service and storing them twice would be wasteful. That is logical and it surprises people badly, because they then have one copy where they assumed two, and a deletion removes it everywhere.

How it fails: nothing looks wrong until something is deleted, at which point it disappears from every device at once and there is no separate copy to fall back on.

The fix: know which one you have. If deleting a photo on your phone removes it from your other devices, that is a sync. Keep a genuine backup alongside it for anything you would be upset to lose.

Less common causes

If none of the six above explain it, these turn up occasionally:

  • Very large files stalling. Multi-gigabyte videos can sit in a queue for a long time, particularly on a slow upload connection, and may look skipped when they are still going.
  • Unsupported or unusual file formats. Some file types produced by third party camera apps are not handled by every service.
  • Odd characters in filenames. Certain symbols cause individual files to fail while everything around them succeeds.
  • An outdated app version. Worth updating before deeper troubleshooting, since some backup faults are fixed in later releases.
  • A second account. Signing in with a different email on a new device is easy to do accidentally, and looks exactly like missing files.
  • The phone being full. Some apps need working room to process an upload queue, so a device with no free space at all can struggle.

The second account case is worth checking early, because it is common, instantly fixable, and produces the alarming version of this problem where a new phone appears to show none of your photos at all.

The habit that catches all of this

Every cause on this page produces the same symptom, which is a backup that looks fine and is not. One habit catches all of them.

Once a month, open your account in a browser and look for something recent. From a computer, not the phone. It takes fifteen seconds and it is the only check that tests the whole chain rather than the app's opinion of itself.

Two moments are worth an extra look:

  • After any major system update, since permissions and battery settings can reset.
  • Before wiping or trading in a phone, when the cost of a gap is permanent.

A backup nobody has verified is an assumption. The distinction only becomes visible on the day it matters, and by then it is too late to do anything about it.

If you do nothing else after reading this, open your library in a browser now and scroll to the oldest photo in it. That one action tells you more than every status screen on your phone.

The whole thing as a checklist

  • Open your account in a browser and check your oldest photos
  • Look specifically for messaging media and screenshots
  • Confirm photo permission is full access, not selected
  • Go through the folder list and enable everything you care about
  • Set the app to unrestricted battery usage and allow background activity
  • Check the last backup date, not just the status word
  • Confirm your account is not full
  • Work out whether you have a backup or a sync
  • Verify the email address signed in matches across devices
  • Repeat the browser check monthly, and after every system update

Nine of those are one-off fixes. The tenth is the one that keeps working, because every cause on this page can come back and none of them will tell you when they do.
